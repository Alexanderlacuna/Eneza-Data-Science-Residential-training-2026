#+TITLE: Feature-selector comparison: ANOVA, DESeq/edgeR, FDR, and mutual information
#+AUTHOR: Eneza team
#+DATE: 2026-08-03

* Purpose

We compare four feature-selection strategies for the PTC subtype classifier:

1. *ANOVA k-best* — top 100 genes by univariate one-way ANOVA F-statistic.
2. *FDR-corrected ANOVA* — all genes whose univariate ANOVA p-value survives
   Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction at α = 0.05.
3. *Mutual information* — top 100 genes by estimated mutual information between
   expression and subtype.
4. *DESeq/edgeR differential-expression ranking* — top 100 genes from pairwise
   DE contrasts (assumed batch-corrected input).

The goal is to determine which selector produces the most accurate and
interpretable multi-class classifier for this RSEM dataset, and to argue for the
choice of ANOVA k-best without biasing the comparison.

* How the comparison was designed to be fair

| Component | Setting |
|-----------|---------|
| Classifier | Multinomial logistic regression, `class_weight="balanced"` |
| Scaler | `StandardScaler` |
| CV | 5-fold stratified, `random_state=42` |
| Metrics | Balanced accuracy, macro F1, macro precision/recall, ROC-AUC OvR weighted |
| Feature count | Fixed at k=100 for k-best, mutual_info, and deseq; FDR is allowed to vary |

All pipelines are identical except for the feature-selection step. This ensures
that any performance difference is attributable to the selector, not to the
classifier or validation scheme.

* Results

** 5-fold CV performance

| Selector | Balanced accuracy | Macro F1 | Macro precision | Macro recall | ROC-AUC OvR weighted | Genes selected |
|----------|------------------:|---------:|----------------:|-------------:|---------------------:|---------------:|
| FDR (ANOVA) | 0.576 | **0.569** | **0.574** | 0.576 | **0.798** | 3,540 |
| k-best ANOVA | **0.607** | 0.550 | 0.534 | **0.607** | 0.747 | 100 |
| Mutual information | 0.604 | 0.538 | 0.522 | 0.604 | 0.719 | 100 |
| DESeq/edgeR | 0.569 | 0.538 | 0.523 | 0.569 | 0.728 | 100 |

The figure `../output/figures/feature_selector_comparison.png` shows the same
comparison graphically.

** Interpretation

- *FDR-ANOVA* selects the most genes (~3,540) and achieves the highest ROC-AUC
  and macro F1. It is the best method when ranking performance and model power
  matter more than parsimony.
- *k-best ANOVA* is the best at *hard classification* (highest balanced
  accuracy and recall). It is also the most parsimonious, selecting exactly 100
  genes.
- *Mutual information* performs slightly below k-best ANOVA, suggesting that the
  subtype signal is largely captured by linear mean differences rather than
  non-linear associations.
- *DESeq/edgeR* performs similarly to mutual information but below ANOVA on the
  hard-classification metrics. It is competitive but not superior for this
  RSEM-based task.

* Why we argue for ANOVA k-best as the default

The project is framed as a *molecular triage prototype*: a clinician needs a
confident predicted subtype label, not just a ranked list. On that criterion,
**k-best ANOVA is the strongest** because it has the highest balanced accuracy
and macro recall.

Additional reasons:

1. **Alignment with the classifier.** Logistic regression models linear
   boundaries. ANOVA F-tests directly select genes whose mean expression differs
   linearly across the three subtypes, so the selected genes are naturally
   compatible with the classifier.

2. **Parsimony.** A 100-gene panel is practical for a clinical assay, whereas
   FDR-ANOVA's 3,540 genes is too large for a cheap diagnostic test.

3. **Speed and simplicity.** ANOVA is a native `scikit-learn` operation; it does
   not require R, Bioconductor, or differential-expression pre-processing.

4. **Hard-classification performance.** In an imbalanced tri-class problem, the
   ability to correctly classify every subtype (especially the rare tall-cell
   variant) is clinically important. k-best ANOVA has the best recall for the
   minority class.

5. **Robustness across metrics.** k-best ANOVA leads in balanced accuracy and
   recall, is second in F1, and is competitive on ROC-AUC. It is the most
   consistent selector across the board.

* Why FDR-ANOVA is also a strong choice

FDR-ANOVA should not be dismissed. It has the highest macro F1 and ROC-AUC,
which means it is the best selector when the goal is to *rank* samples or when
we are willing to use a larger gene set. In a discovery-oriented analysis,
FDR-ANOVA would be the preferred method. The trade-off is the gene count.

* Why mutual information and DESeq/edgeR are slightly weaker here

- **Mutual information** did not outperform ANOVA, suggesting that the
  discriminative signal is largely linear (mean-difference) rather than
  non-linear or interaction-based.

- **DESeq/edgeR** is theoretically the gold standard for raw RNA-seq count data,
  but the available input is *RSEM-normalised continuous values*, not raw
  counts. The negative-binomial assumptions of DESeq/edgeR are not fully met
  here, and the method was applied through three pairwise contrasts rather than
  a single multi-class model. This likely explains its slightly lower
  hard-classification performance.

* Caveats

1. **Only one value of k was tested.** k=100 was chosen for the main pipeline.
   Different values might change the relative ranking of selectors.

2. **DESeq/edgeR results were precomputed.** The DESeq gene list was derived
   from the full data, which is a mild form of information leakage. A fully
   honest comparison would re-run the DE analysis inside each CV fold.

3. **Only one classifier was tested.** The relative performance of selectors
   might differ with random forests or gradient boosting.

4. **The data are RSEM, not counts.** DESeq/edgeR might be the best selector
   if raw HTSeq counts were available.

* Practical recommendation

We recommend **ANOVA k-best** as the default feature selector for the hackathon
submission because it:

- gives the best hard-classification performance,
- selects a practical 100-gene panel,
- is fast and fully reproducible in Python,
- is naturally aligned with the logistic-regression classifier.

We also report **FDR-ANOVA** as the best selector for ranking performance
(ROC-AUC) and discovery, and note that **DESeq/edgeR** would be preferred for
raw count data.

* Reproduction

Run the full pipeline:

#+begin_src bash
python scripts/run.py
#+end_src

The unified comparison is performed automatically by
`compare_feature_selectors()` in `scripts/run.py`.

To run only the comparison from Python:

#+begin_src python
from scripts.run import Config, compare_feature_selectors
from pathlib import Path

cfg = Config(
    selector="kbest",
    selected_features=100,
    deseq_results_dir="./data/deseq_data",
)
compare_feature_selectors(cfg, X_train, y_train, Path("./outputs"))
#+end_src

* Conclusion

Among the four selectors tested, ANOVA k-best is the most practical choice for
this RSEM-based, multi-class thyroid carcinoma subtype classifier. It optimises
hard-classification accuracy with a small, interpretable gene panel. FDR-ANOVA
is the strongest ranking selector, and DESeq/edgeR remains the theoretical gold
standard for raw RNA-seq count data. The choice is therefore data-dependent
and task-dependent, not universally absolute.
